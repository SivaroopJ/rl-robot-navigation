## E-a — shadow, per step

The frozen solver drives the loop. At every step the fast solver solves the identical inputs
(p, γ, ξ, u_prev), where u_prev is the frozen controller's previous action as Random recovery
left it. Categories follow what the policy does with a status: *optimal* (action used),
*infeasible* (Random recovery), *other* (frozen u = 0).

Interpretations fixed in code before the run (disclosed here, not deviations from a rule):
- §8 says "feasibility category (optimal vs infeasible)". Statuses that are neither (e.g.
  `optimal_inaccurate`, `solver_error`) form a third category, *other*, because the policy treats
  them differently from both. This is stricter than a two-way split: it can only move the
  decision towards frozen SCS.
- E-a is decided on the corpus pooled over both arms and both conditions (§8: "driving arm 1
  and arm 2"); per-arm and per-condition rows are descriptive.
- Steps with h_crit < 0 are delegated by the fast solver to the frozen controller and agree by
  construction: 2 such steps in the corpus.

## Addendum (written after the run; descriptive, changes no rule or verdict)

What the 366 category disagreements are, read from the E-a traces:

| arm | frozen status | fast status | steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| floor | optimal | solver_error (OSQP iteration cap, 20 000) | 256 |
| floor | optimal | optimal_inaccurate | 52 |
| ceiling | optimal | solver_error | 50 |
| ceiling | optimal | optimal_inaccurate | 7 |
| floor | optimal_inaccurate | optimal | 1 |

- **The E-a failure does not depend on the three-way category.** In 365 of the 366 steps, the
  frozen solver is optimal and the fast solver is not.
  - Under the literal two-way "optimal vs infeasible" wording these steps still disagree.
  - Even folding `optimal_inaccurate` into *optimal* would leave the 306 `solver_error` steps.
- **What those steps do to the policy.** On a `solver_error` step the fast policy commands the
  frozen fallback u = 0 where the frozen controller had an optimal action. That is a behavioural
  difference, not a tolerance question.
- **The closed loop did not detect it at this sample size.** E-b passed for both arms: success
  moved by −0.005 in each arm. The pre-registered rule needs E-a *and* E-b, so the decision is
  **frozen SCS for training**.
- **The fast solver is not modified**, including its OSQP settings (§8, spec "Out of scope").
- **Cost** (spec: ~90 CPU-hours for 3 × 3M steps on frozen SCS). Frozen SCS takes about
  27.5 ms per QP against about 0.5 ms for the fast solver.
